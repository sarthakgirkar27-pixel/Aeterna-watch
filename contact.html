    The atelier

    One room, four benches, one floor up.

    The whole house is one room in Plainpalais, over a locksmith, with four benches along the window. There is no boutique, no counter and no sign on the street door. You ring the middle bell and one of us comes down.

    Visits are by appointment, and only by appointment

    Four people build every watch we sell. If two of them are downstairs talking, the day's work stops, so we keep visits to three afternoons a week and book them one at a time. Ask in the form above, or telephone and speak to whoever picks up. We usually have something inside ten days.

    What forty five minutes gets you

    Two references out of the safe and onto a cloth, in daylight, on your wrist. A movement out of its case under a loupe so you can see what 312 parts looks like. Ten minutes at whichever bench is free, watching one being regulated. Then coffee, and the honest conversation about which case makes sense for you, including the one where we tell you to keep your money.

    Nobody will ask you to decide anything on the day. We do not take orders in the room, because a 44 mm watch you have just held for the first time is not a decision to make in front of the person who made it.

    • Send back the watch, both straps, the certificate and the box. If the box is damaged in your house, say so and it costs you nothing.

    When a refund clears we update the register that day and the piece number is freed for the next owner, with your name removed. A returned watch is sold as new only if it has been fully refinished and re-regulated, and the register says so either way.

    Five years from the day the watch is delivered, and it moves with the watch. If you sell it, the new owner has whatever is left, and changing the name in the register costs nothing. We are one of very few houses that will do that without an inspection fee.

    The warranty covers the movement, the case, the dial, the hands and the crystal against anything that is our fault: a part that fails, a finish that lifts, a rate that drifts outside the tolerance we printed on your certificate.

    • Not covered: water past 100 metres, a crystal broken by an impact, a case damaged in a fall, or a strap worn out by wearing it. Straps are consumables. A replacement is $420 and takes about a week.
    • Also not covered: a movement that has been opened by somebody else. We say this without pleasure, but a chronograph with 312 parts does not forgive an amateur, and we cannot stand behind work we did not do.

    Service interval is about six to eight years, not five. If the watch keeps time inside its tolerance and still runs past sixty hours off a full wind, leave it alone. Opening a healthy movement on a calendar is a service industry habit, not watchmaking.

    What this website does

    It collects nothing. There is no analytics, no tag manager, no advertising pixel, no session recorder and no A/B testing tool. No request leaves your browser carrying anything about you, because there is nowhere for it to go.

    One request does go to another company. The two typefaces come from Google Fonts, which means Google's servers see your IP address and your browser's user agent when a page loads. We would rather self host them and we have not yet. Everything else, the film, the photographs, the stylesheets and the scripts, is served from this domain.

    The forms on this site do not send anything anywhere. The enquiry form above checks your answers in your browser and then stops. There is no endpoint behind it.

    We are aiming at WCAG 2.2 level AA and we are not claiming to have reached it. Below is what this site actually does, and then what we know is still wrong. The second list is the honest part.

    What works today

    • Everything is reachable with a keyboard, in the order it appears on the page. A skip link is the first stop on every page. Focus shows as a solid two pixel outline held three pixels clear of the control, on paper and on the dark sections.
    • One h1 per page, headings in order with no levels skipped, and real landmarks: a banner, a main, a footer and named navigation.
    • Body text measures 8.3:1 against the paper it sits on. The lowest contrast anywhere on this page is 4.7:1, on the small gold numerals beside the four steps in the enquiry panel, against a target of 4.5:1. Borders and large type are held above 3:1, and colour is never the only thing telling you two states apart.
    • With prefers-reduced-motion set to reduce, the scroll driven film on the home page is replaced by a still photograph, entrance transitions do not run, and no element is left invisible or part way through a movement. Nothing on the site autoplays with sound.
    • Controls are at least 44 by 44 pixels under a touch pointer, and the forms use real labels, real fieldsets and per field error messages tied to their inputs rather than colour alone.
    • With JavaScript switched off every page is still a complete document. Links work, all the policy text above is visible at once, and the forms tell you to write to the atelier instead.

    What is not right yet

    • The home page opens on a film that advances as you scroll. It respects the reduced motion setting, but for a reader who finds motion difficult and has not set that preference, it is a demanding way to start.
    • Links that sit inside a sentence, like the email address a few paragraphs up, are only as tall as the line they are in, which measures about 20 pixels. The target size rule exempts a link inside a block of text, so this passes, but it is still a small thing to hit on a moving train and we would rather it were not.
    • The plan of the atelier further up this page is a drawing. Everything in it is written out in the paragraphs beside it, so nothing is lost, but it is not a described diagram.
    • We have tested by hand at 320, 375, 768, 1024 and 1440 pixels, with a keyboard, with reduced motion on and with JavaScript off. We have not yet sat down with somebody who uses a screen reader daily, and until we have we will not tell you the site is conformant.

    Ask us the eighth

    Yes. 18k gold is softer than steel and softer again than the titanium references, and the first mark usually arrives inside the first month, generally on the left side of the case from a door frame or a desk edge. Anybody who tells you otherwise is selling you something.

    What we do about it: every service includes a full case refinish at no charge, for the life of the watch, and we will refinish a case on its own for nothing if you are passing Geneva. A rose gold case can be brought back to the day it shipped perhaps six or seven times before the chamfers start to soften, which is more lifetimes than most watches get.

    If a mark on a case would genuinely bother you, buy the titanium. It is $15,500 less, it marks less readily, and the movement inside it is identical. We have talked more people into titanium than out of it.

    Solid, all the way through, cast in our own foundry and hallmarked inside the caseback where you can see it with the movement out. The rose gold is 18k. The king gold is an 18k alloy with about five percent platinum, which holds a deeper red and dulls more slowly. Nothing on the outside of any Aeterna is plated, coated or filled.

    The titanium references are Grade 5 throughout, including the crown, the pushers and the clasp. No steel parts, no coated steel pretending to be titanium.

    One honest exception. Inside Sovereign the movement bridges are gilded rather than rhodium plated, so the gold runs through the watch instead of around it. Gilding is a plated finish, it is on a part you look at through the crystal, and it is the only plating in the collection. We would rather write that here than have you find it in a forum.

    Because the two materials do different jobs. The strap is alligator on the outside over vulcanised rubber underneath. The rubber takes the sweat, the sun cream and the sea water. The alligator takes the looking at. Neither one would survive doing the other's work.

    A lined leather strap on a 44 mm gold watch, worn in summer by somebody with warm wrists, is a strap you replace twice a year and a watch that smells of it in between. We tried it for the first two years of the reference and stopped.

    Replacements are $420 and take about a week, or nothing at all if you are in Geneva and can wait twenty minutes. Straps are the one part of this watch we treat as a consumable, and we say so in the warranty rather than in the small print.

    $980 for a full service. That number is printed on your certificate on the day you buy the watch and it does not move for the life of the watch, so a service in 2044 costs $980 whatever has happened to the franc in between. A crystal is $310. A strap change while you wait is free. A case refinish is included in every service, always.

    The interval is about six to eight years rather than the five you may be used to hearing. If the watch is inside its rate tolerance and still runs past sixty hours from a full wind, leave it alone. We will tell you to come back later if you bring it in early, and we will not charge you for the conversation.

    Turnaround is about six weeks, and we will lend you a Frost while yours is with us so there is nothing on your wrist that you have to think about.

    We do not know, and we are not going to pretend to. We have been making watches since 1948 and we cannot tell you what a Chronographe 312 will fetch in 2040. Anyone who can is guessing with more confidence than we have.

    What is true and checkable: there are 312 of them and there will never be more, the register is public so provenance is never in doubt, and service costs are fixed in writing, which removes the usual reason a small house watch becomes unsellable. Those things tend to help.

    What is also true: we are a small house with four employees and a thin secondary market. A thin market moves in both directions and it moves further than a big one. If the resale number is central to your decision, this is the wrong watch, and there is no version of this answer where we tell you otherwise.
